ALMM List-III rule: solar makers must buy wafers and ingots made in India

Why that last rule matters

If wafers and ingots must be made in India, someone has to supply the raw silicon for them. Big Indian solar firms are already building ingot and wafer plants. Evoxia’s pitch is simple: be the Indian supplier of that raw silicon, so those plants do not need to import it.

What Evoxia does and how

Evoxia turns quartz into solar-grade silicon. The standard industry route is long and chemical-heavy. Evoxia claims a shorter route that uses a very hot reactor to pull the oxygen out of quartz directly.

Analyst notes: what to check next

These are my observations from reading the deck, not claims by Evoxia. They are good questions for a diligence call.

What is strong. A real policy tailwind (ALMM List-III), named buyers, a clear cost story, credible advisors, and a small first raise.
Proof of purity. The spec table states 7N as achieved, but the roadmap lists ICP-MS data showing at least 7N as a milestone for July to Dec 2026. Ask for the lab reports today.
Reactor size vs output. The pilot plans a 100 kW plasma reactor for 1,000 t a year. At the claimed 11 kWh/kg, 1,000 t needs about 11 GWh a year, roughly 1.3 MW of continuous power. 100 kW would give roughly 80 t a year at full-time running. Worth asking how this reconciles (my own arithmetic).
Funding gap. Pilot plant funding is ₹10.2 Cr and the ask is ₹15 Cr, but the demo plant (7,200 t) and commercial plant (14,400 t) need far more. The deck’s own article cites about ₹850 Cr per GW for polysilicon plus metallurgical silicon, against Evoxia’s $7M per 10,000 t claim.
Inconsistent China share. The deck says over 94%, one slide shows 63%, another shows 85 to 90%. The measure (production, capacity, year) differs. Tidy this before relying on it.
Revenue jump. ₹0.24 Cr to ₹48.79 Cr in three years, with the pilot at 1,000 t. Check the assumed price (₹500/kg), volumes and yield. Also, the demo (7,200 t) would be bigger than the FY28-29 revenue implies.
Buyers not yet committed in writing. “Explicitly committed” in Tier 1 is not an LOI; the plan lists the first LOI as a 2027 milestone. RSOLEC is also listed as targeting first product in early 2026.
Technology detail. The deck calls the process “proprietary high-temperature reduction” and mentions plasma, but gives no reactant, yield or IP status (patents are still to be filed). Zero toxic by-products also deserves a technical check.
